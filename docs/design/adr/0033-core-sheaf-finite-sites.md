# ADR-0033: `core.sheaf` — one sheaf-theory facility on finite sites

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Scope:** the representation of finite sites, sieves, presheaves and gluing
used by every Eshkol program that computes with sheaves; the exact linear
algebra over Z that the abelian layer rests on; the boundary with
`core.topos` (internal language and forcing, ADR-0035).

## Context

The mathematics examples shipped with v1.3.6 compute sheafification, sheaf
cohomology of finite spaces, Cech descent, hypercohomology, stacks and the
internal logic of presheaf toposes. Each program rebuilt the same machinery
on top of `(require stdlib)`: finite posets with their transitive closure,
finite categories from presentations, sieves and the three Grothendieck
axioms, presheaves with restriction tables, matching families, the plus
construction, Cech complexes, and an integer Smith normal form. The Smith
normal form alone appears in fifteen example programs, and the category,
sieve and topology code in three. Each copy is a separate place for a
convention to drift (composition order, the orientation of restriction
matrices, the enumeration order of covers), and none of it is reachable from
a program that wants to glue local results rather than reproduce a theorem.

The design goal is that Eshkol's own vocabulary is Grothendieck's: a site is
a value, a covering is a decidable predicate on sieves, the sheaf condition is
an operation that returns the global section or names the overlaps where
gluing fails, and cohomology measures the failure exactly.

## Decision

1. **One library, `core.sheaf`, in layers.** `core.sheaf.site` (categories,
   sieves, topologies, sites, finite spaces), `core.sheaf.glue` (gluing
   results and covers of data), `core.sheaf.presheaf` (presheaves of sets),
   `core.sheaf.module` (presheaves of free Z-modules and Q-vector spaces),
   `core.sheaf.cohomology` (Cech cohomology, sheaves on finite spaces by
   stalks, derived limits) and `core.integer_linalg` (Smith normal form,
   lattices, cochain cohomology). `(require core.sheaf)` loads all of them.
   They are ordinary Eshkol modules loaded by `require`, outside the
   `(require stdlib)` closure, so the VM prelude and its generated binding
   tables are unchanged.

2. **Sites are finite categories with a covering predicate.** A category is
   `#(finite-category nobj arrows compose ids labels)` with integer objects
   and indexed arrows; `compose` is a procedure, so presented categories (a
   hash table of composites) and thin categories (an index table on pairs)
   share one interface. A sieve is an ascending list of arrow indices. A site
   is `#(site category covering? minimal)`: the topology is a decision
   procedure, never a mandatory list of covering sieves, so a site does not
   pay for enumerating its sieves until an operation quantifies over them.
   `minimal` is an optional basis giving the smallest covering sieve (the
   finite-space site supplies the sieve generated by the minimal opens).
   `grothendieck-topology-violations` checks the three axioms of SGA 4 II.1.1
   and names each failure. Finite spaces are a constructor of sites, not a
   separate theory: `finite-space-site` builds the category of opens with the
   union covering predicate.

3. **The sheaf condition is gluing.** Every gluing operation returns
   `#(gluing glued value)` or `#(gluing obstructed (obstruction ...))`, with
   obstructions `(disagree i j overlap vi vj)`, `(incomplete i missing)`,
   `(incompatible f g)`, `(no-amalgamation)` and `(not-unique n)`. The same
   result type serves set-valued families on sieves (`glue-family`), module
   families (`module-glue`) and data split over pieces (`glue-cover`,
   `glue-keyed-sections`), so a distributed computation and a theorem about
   a site report failure in the same vocabulary. `make-cover` is general (any
   pieces, overlap, restriction and equality); nothing in it names a mesh,
   device or storage system.

4. **One plus construction per coefficient kind, computed at the smallest
   covering sieve.** On a finite site the covering sieves of an object are
   closed under intersection, so `colim_R Match(R, F)` is attained at
   `R_min = ∩ J(c)` and `F^+(c) = Match(R_min(c), F)`. Families are always
   taken on sieves; a cover given as a family of arrows is tested through the
   sieve it generates (`module-sheaf-on-family?`), which is equivalent for
   presheaves (Mac Lane–Moerdijk III.4) and removes the special case of
   pairwise-intersection equalizers.

5. **Exact coefficients over Z, with Q by clearing denominators.** Module
   presheaves carry `ring` `'Z` or `'Q`. Over Z the sheaf condition is an
   isomorphism of lattices decided by the Smith normal form, so torsion is
   visible in cohomology; over Q each row is scaled by the least common
   multiple of its denominators using integer products only, so Q-ranks and
   Q-kernels come from the same integer engine and rational arithmetic is
   used only for final coordinates, each checked to stay exact.
   `core.integer_linalg` uses the matrix representation of
   `core.exact_linalg` (vectors of row vectors) and owns the PID algorithms
   that `core.exact_linalg` (a field library) does not.

6. **One Cech engine.** `cech-complex-from-nerve` takes the nerve data (the
   intersection of an index set, the rank of a section group, the restriction
   matrix) and builds the complex. Module presheaves on thin sites with meets
   and sheaves on finite spaces given by their stalks are two front ends of
   that engine. Derived limits use the cosimplicial replacement over chains.
   `cochain-cohomology-generators` returns explicit cocycles adapted to the
   Smith form, so a nonzero class always comes with a witness.

7. **Representation is public and stable.** The site, sieve, presheaf and
   restriction API is documented in `docs/reference/stdlib/sheaf.md` and is
   the base of `core.topos` (ADR-0035), which adds the internal language and
   forcing without re-encoding sites.

## Consequences

- The finite-site sheafification and finite-space cohomology examples are
  written against the library; their output is byte-identical to the
  stand-alone programs and they shrink from 364 and 398 lines to 168 and
  185. The other sheaf, descent and cohomology examples can move the same
  way; each move is checked by an identical-output diff.
- Loading the whole library costs JIT compilation time on every run; a
  program can require only the layers it uses.
- Quantifying over all sieves enumerates subsets of the arrows into an
  object, which bounds that use to small sites. Covering predicates, the
  minimal-sieve basis and Cech complexes of explicit covers keep larger
  finite spaces usable.
- Coefficients are free modules over Z or Q. Sheaves of finitely generated
  modules with torsion stalks, sheaves of groupoids (stacks) and
  hypercohomology of complexes are Planned for v1.4.1 on the same
  representation.

## Alternatives considered

- **A topology as an explicit list of covering sieves only.** Simple, but it
  forces enumeration of every sieve before any use and makes the site of a
  16-point finite space impractical. Kept as a constructor
  (`make-site-from-sieves`) over the predicate form.
- **Separate code paths for open covers of finite spaces and sieves of
  general sites.** This is what the examples did; it duplicates the sheaf
  condition and lets the two disagree. Families now go through generated
  sieves.
- **Adding the Smith normal form to `core.exact_linalg`.** That module is in
  the `(require stdlib)` closure and is a field library; integer lattice
  algorithms belong beside it, not inside it, and keeping them outside the
  closure leaves the VM prelude unchanged.
- **Floating-point ranks.** Rejected: ranks and torsion are exact
  invariants; every result here is exact.

## Verification

- `tests/stdlib/sheaf_test.esk` (CTest `sheaf_jit`, `sheaf_aot`): Smith forms
  verified by `U A V = D` and the divisibility chain; ranks and kernels
  cross-checked against `core.exact_linalg` over Q; the three topology axioms
  with deliberately invalid topologies; sheafification against the
  `|A|^{π0(U)}` formula computed from components; idempotence; gluing
  successes and every obstruction kind; Q-coefficient presheaves; derived
  cohomology of the pseudocircle (`H^1 = Z`), its twisted local system
  (`H^1 = Z/2` with an order-2 cocycle witness), the sphere and torus models;
  agreement of two independent Cech paths; Cech equal to derived cohomology
  on a Leray cover and different (`H^2`) on a non-Leray cover of the
  pseudo-2-sphere; Euler characteristics.
- The two rewritten examples (CTest `sheaf_finite_site_sheafification_*`,
  `sheaf_finite_spaces_cech_descent_*`) diffed byte for byte against the
  previous programs.
