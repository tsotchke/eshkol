# ADR-0039: Algebraic theories as data: Lawvere theories and their models

- **Status:** Proposed — library implemented in `core.theory`
- **Date:** 2026-10-29
- **Decision owners:** `core.ring`, `core.topos`, `core.category` and exact algebra maintainers
- **Depends on:** ADR-0034 (ring carriers), ADR-0035 (Kripke-Joyal semantics, `core.topos`), ADR-0033 (finite sites), ADR-0036 (`core.category`)
- **Scope:** library level (`lib/core/theory.esk`, `lib/core/theory/`) and the law check of `core.ring`; no compiler, runtime or VM change
- **Evidence:** `tests/stdlib/theory_test.esk` (84 checks, 134749 law evaluations), `tests/stdlib/theory_topos_test.esk`, the examples on `docs/reference/stdlib/theory.md`, on native JIT and AOT

## Context

ADR-0034 made a commutative ring a value: a carrier descriptor holding its
operations, with a law gate that checks the ring laws as equalities on
samples. The same shape recurs across the library and the mathematics
examples: groups given by multiplication tables (the group cohomology
examples each define them), lattices and Heyting algebras of sieves
(`core.topos`), Boolean algebras of truth values, modules. Each has its own
operations and its own ad hoc axiom check, and nothing relates them: a ring
is not seen as an abelian group under addition, a Boolean algebra is not seen
as a Heyting algebra, and the ring laws are written once in `core.ring`'s
loop and nowhere else.

Universal algebra and Lawvere's functorial semantics give the common
structure. An algebraic theory is a signature with equational laws; its
models in sets are the algebras; and the theory itself is a category L_T
with finite products, whose morphisms n -> m are m-tuples of terms in n
variables modulo the laws, so that a model is a product-preserving functor
L_T -> Set and a morphism of theories is a product-preserving functor
between Lawvere theories (Lawvere 1963; Adamek, Rosicky and Vitale 2011;
nLab "Lawvere theory").

## Decision

### 1. One representation of theories, terms and models

`core.theory.equational` (no dependencies) represents:

- a **signature** as a list of `(operation arity)`; operation names are any
  data compared with `equal?`, so parameterised families (the scalars
  `(scale r)` of a module) are signatures too;
- a **term** as a variable (a symbol) or an application `(op t ...)`;
- an **equation** as a name and two terms; a **theory** as a name, a
  signature, equations and a *kind* naming its decision procedure;
- a **model** as a carrier given by its operations, its equality, and, when
  finite, the list of its canonical elements.

### 2. One law check, which core.ring's law gate uses

The law check evaluates every equation under every assignment of the samples
(every element of a finite model), extending assignments one variable at a
time and checking at each prefix the equations with exactly that many
variables. A violation is `(law value ...)`. This is the loop `core.ring`'s
`ring-law-violations` ran, generalised: `commutative-ring-theory` lists the
eight ring laws in the gate's order, `ring-carrier->model` (in `core.ring`)
presents any carrier as a model of it, and `ring-law-violations` now checks
the ring laws by this law check and keeps only the carrier protocol's own
checks (the unit group, `from-integer` as a ring map, the characteristic).
Carriers, their descriptors and every call site are unchanged; the ring
suites print the same output. Ring carriers became models through an
adapter, not a rewrite.

### 3. Constructions on finite models

Homomorphisms are checked on every tuple; all homomorphisms between finite
models are found from the images of a generating set, extended along the
operations and kept when well defined. Products are componentwise;
congruences are checked one argument position at a time, generated by
union-find closed under unary polynomials, and quotients are refused unless
the partition is a congruence. Interpretations S -> T (each S-operation a
T-term) translate terms and give reducts (forgetful functors); an
interpretation is a morphism of theories exactly when T proves every
translated S-law, which the word problem decides.

### 4. The word problem, solved for the catalogue

`core.theory.free` builds the free model on finite generators for every kind
in the catalogue, with an equality that decides the word problem: words
(semigroups, monoids), freely reduced words (groups), Z^n (abelian groups),
Z<X> as `core.dbsp` Z-sets (rings), Z[X] as `core.ring`'s monoid ring
(commutative rings), Whitman's conditions (lattices), truth tables
(distributive lattices and Boolean algebras, varieties generated by their
two-element algebra), Dyckhoff's terminating sequent calculus G4ip (Heyting
algebras, by the correspondence with intuitionistic propositional logic) and
R^n (modules over a finite ring). A theory with equations and no decision
procedure is refused by name, never approximated.

### 5. The Lawvere theory

`core.theory.lawvere` gives L_T symbolically: morphisms are term tuples,
composition is substitution, n + m is the product with variable projections,
and equality of morphisms is the word problem. A model acts as a functor by
evaluation; a morphism n -> m acts contravariantly as the homomorphism of free
models F(m) -> F(n), so L_T is the opposite of finitely generated free models.
When free models are finite, the truncation to objects 0 .. N is a finite
category in the `core.sheaf.site` representation that `core.category`
(ADR-0036) builds on, so the category laws and constructions there apply
without a second category type.

### 6. Finite models and the topos connection

`core.theory.finite` provides the fourteen groups of order at most eight
(with the D4 and Q8 encodings of the group cohomology examples), Z/n through
`core.ring`, matrix rings, modules, finite lattices, Heyting and Boolean
algebras. `core.theory.topos` presents Omega_J(c) of a `core.topos` topos as a
model of `heyting-algebra-theory` with `core.topos`'s own operations, and
restriction of sieves as Heyting homomorphisms.

### 7. Coalgebras, where finite

Finite coalgebras for F X = O x X^A (Moore machines) are the dual case:
coalgebra homomorphisms, the largest bisimulation by partition refinement,
and the minimal coalgebra as the quotient by it.

## Alternatives rejected

- **One record type per structure (groups, lattices, ...).** It repeats the
  law check, homomorphisms and quotients per structure and leaves the
  relations between structures (a ring is an abelian group, a Boolean algebra
  is a Heyting algebra) unexpressed.
- **A second ring-law loop in `core.theory`.** Two checks of the same laws
  could drift; `core.ring` now runs the shared check.
- **Knuth-Bendix completion as the only word-problem solver.** Completion
  does not terminate for some presentations; the catalogue's theories have
  known decision procedures that always terminate.
